Additional Strategic, Product, Operational, Trust, and Scale Requirements for Unns

You are operating as an elite cross-functional venture studio composed of senior experts in product strategy, behavioral psychology, AI systems, platform architecture, security, privacy, DevOps, analytics, growth, support operations, and digital trust.

Your responsibility is to perform a full secondary review of the Unns product vision and execution scope in order to identify all major requirements, systems, workflows, controls, and capabilities that may still be missing, underdeveloped, or insufficiently specified.

You must not repeat previously covered items unless necessary for integration and completeness.
Instead, your role is to identify and define the additional critical requirements that must exist in order for Unns to become a truly world-class, scalable, trustworthy, and category-leading emotional operating system.

If any of the following areas have not already been implemented or specified properly, they must now be added, designed, and structured as part of the product blueprint.

Do not think narrowly.
Think as if Unns is expected to become the market leader in the Arab world, expand regionally, serve both consumers and institutions, and operate under high expectations of trust, performance, privacy, safety, and emotional quality.

⸻

1) First Mandatory Step: Conduct a Gap Review Before Adding Anything

Before proposing or building anything, first audit the current product scope and determine:
	•	what has already been covered sufficiently
	•	what is only partially defined
	•	what is missing completely
	•	what needs strengthening for scale, trust, or monetization
	•	what may become a bottleneck later if ignored now

Then expand the product requirements accordingly.

Do not assume completeness.
Do not assume that core product screens alone are enough.
Do not assume that a strong AI companion by itself guarantees long-term success.

2) Onboarding, User Intake, and Personal Baseline System

Design a complete onboarding and baseline understanding system.

This must include:
	•	account creation flow
	•	anonymous or lightweight entry options if appropriate
	•	emotional baseline questions
	•	user goals selection
	•	preferred support style
	•	preferred interaction format:
	•	text
	•	voice
	•	guided activities
	•	silent reflection
	•	language preference
	•	notification preference setup
	•	privacy and consent acceptance
	•	expectation setting for what Unns is and is not
	•	safe disclosure and trust-building copy during onboarding

The onboarding must not feel long, clinical, or heavy.
It must feel emotionally intelligent, elegant, and low-friction.

It should also create the initial personalization foundation for the AI companion, nudges, daily loop, and progress engine.

3) Consent, Privacy Transparency, and Trust Center

Because Unns deals with emotionally sensitive interactions, build an explicit trust framework.

This must include:
	•	consent flows for data use
	•	consent for AI interaction where applicable
	•	clear explanation of what data is collected
	•	clear explanation of how data is used
	•	explanation of what is stored and what is not
	•	user control over data retention
	•	data export request flow
	•	account deletion flow
	•	conversation deletion options where appropriate
	•	privacy center inside the app
	•	trust center on the landing page and inside product settings
	•	clear boundaries between wellness support and medical care
	•	transparent explanation of AI limitations
	•	user-facing explanation of moderation and safety safeguards

Trust must be designed as a product feature, not hidden inside legal pages only.

4) Crisis, Safety Escalation, and Sensitive-State Handling

Unns must have a carefully designed response framework for users in distress.

You must define and structure:
	•	crisis phrase detection
	•	self-harm or high-risk language detection logic
	•	escalation rules
	•	region-appropriate emergency guidance
	•	safe fallback responses
	•	de-escalation response templates
	•	“seek professional help” flows
	•	local resource presentation where possible
	•	safety banners or prompts
	•	internal logging and review procedures
	•	differentiation between:
	•	mild distress
	•	urgent distress
	•	crisis-risk content

The system must never behave recklessly, passively, or ambiguously in high-risk moments.

It must also avoid pretending to be a clinical authority when it is not.
